Practice Areas

Three ways I work with organisations.

Building the product, engineering it as a business, or advising the team that owns it — each is a distinct kind of work, and each is something I do regularly.

App Development

Applications built for the place the work actually happens — a clinic counter, a field visit, a shop floor, or a customer's phone. Designed to stay usable on poor connections and inexpensive hardware.

  • Cross-platform mobile from a single codebase, so one build serves both iOS and Android
  • Native iOS and Android where platform capability or performance demands it
  • Progressive web apps that install to the home screen, work offline, and need no app store
  • Web application front-ends — dashboards, admin panels, and internal operational tools
Typical starting point: a working prototype of the core screen and data flow before a full build commitment.

SaaS Product Engineering

Full product builds, from the first data model to a system that runs without me. Multi-tenant from the start, so the second customer does not require a rewrite of the first.

  • Multi-tenant architecture with strict data isolation and per-account permissions
  • API and database design that stays fast as tables grow into millions of rows
  • Roles, billing-ready models, notifications, and audit trails built in from the beginning
  • Cloud deployment and operations — containers, Linux servers, and predictable running costs
Evidence: TaskEdge, a multi-tenant operations SaaS, and iCare, a multi-tenant health platform across two states.

Consulting & Advisory

For teams that already have engineers and need an outside read on architecture, delivery, or a system that has become difficult to change safely.

  • Architecture and code review — what is load-bearing, what is risk, what to fix first
  • System design for new products, migrations, and integrations with government or partner systems
  • Technology selection grounded in what the team can maintain after I leave
  • Delivery guidance — scoping, sequencing, review standards, and technical roadmaps
Who it suits: founders, engineering leads, and non-technical stakeholders who need a straight technical answer.

SaaS · Multi-tenant · Healthcare

iCare

A digital patient registry and medicine inventory platform for hemophilia and thalassemia care, deployed across treatment centres in Punjab and Madhya Pradesh.

  • Multi-tenant across states: separate treatment centres share reporting while keeping patient data isolated.
  • Cross-hospital lookup: a patient registered at one centre receives verified treatment at any connected centre.

Working Together

How an engagement runs.

The same sequence whether the work is a full product build or a focused advisory review. Scope and depth change; the order does not.

Step 01
Conversation
A short call or exchange about the problem, the constraints, and what success looks like. No cost, and often enough to answer the question outright.
Step 02
Scope
We agree what is being built or reviewed, what is explicitly out of scope, and the shape of the engagement, in writing before work starts.
Step 03
Architecture
Data model, interfaces, and deployment decided and documented first, so implementation is execution rather than discovery.
Step 04
Build & hand over
Delivered in working increments with review at each step, plus the documentation and runbook the team needs to run it without me.
